1 · Purpose and position
Sanctions compliance is absolute in character: obligations apply regardless of intent, knowledge
thresholds are low, and a single missed listed party is a breach. This policy states which regimes
bind Mynta, how exposure is found, and what happens on a match. It is deliberately short; the
operational weight lives in SOP-12 and SOP-21, and the ownership structure-quality dependency
lives in SOP-10, per GOV-01 conclusion C4.


2 · Legal basis and list regime
Binding: EU restrictive measures, directly applicable as regulations, including asset freezes and
sectoral prohibitions; UN measures as implemented in EU law; Swedish national measures under lag
(1996:95) om vissa internationella sanktioner. Supervision and enforcement sit with
Finansinspektionen and prosecuting authorities.

Risk-based additional screening: designated OFAC programmes. Three different things are kept apart
here. What binds Mynta as law is the EU and Swedish framework above; US sanctions do not apply
to Mynta directly absent a US connection. What exists nonetheless is secondary-sanctions

3 · Definitions

Listed person              A natural or legal person designated under a regime in section 2, or treated as
                           designated under section 5.

Freeze                     Prevention of any move, transfer, alteration, use of, access to or dealing with
                           funds and economic resources.

Making available           Any provision of funds, services or economic resources, direct or indirect,
                           including through intermediaries.

Circumvention              Participation in activities whose object or effect is to defeat a measure, including
                           knowingly enabling structures used for that purpose.

True match                 Per C-4: a confirmed match, or a hit that cannot be dismissed on evidence.



4 · Sectoral and territorial measures
Beyond list-based freezes, regimes in force impose sectoral prohibitions (finance, energy, defence,
dual-use and listed goods) and territorial restrictions. Mynta's exposure to these is limited by its
business model: no trade finance, no correspondent activity, no cash. The residual exposure paths
are: acquiring merchants whose trade touches restricted sectors or dual-use goods; cross-border
payments referencing restricted goods, services or territories; and the crypto Crypto Service’s
counterparty exposure. The relevant prohibitions include the territorial restrictions in force (occupied
and embargoed territories) and the sectoral measures on finance, energy, defence and dual-use
items. Controls per channel: SOP-15 underwriting and C-7 category governance ask the sectoral
questions before a merchant is signed, not after; C-2 prohibited-band enforcement applies at any
level of the ownership structure; SOP-18 cross-border flow and reference screening runs per the
register, and payment-reference screening alone is not treated as a reliable dual-use control; every
restricted-sector indication escalates to the MLRO before execution, and permit questions follow
section 9. Where a sectoral question cannot be resolved from the regime text, external counsel shall
be engaged before the transaction proceeds.


5 · Prohibitions
  • No funds or economic resources are made available, directly or indirectly, to or for the benefit of a
    listed person or an entity owned or controlled by one.
  • Frozen assets are not dealt with, moved or released except under a licence or exemption lawfully
    obtained.
  • No participation in, and no facilitation of, circumvention: structures, routings or renamings whose
    purpose or effect is to defeat measures are treated as the measure applying.

6 · Ownership and control: the 50% rule
The EU and US rules differ and are applied separately. Under the EU framework, ownership and
control are two separate tests: an entity owned 50% or more, in total, by listed persons is treated as
listed, and so is an entity a listed person controls, and control can exist below 50%. Under OFAC's 50
Percent Rule, total ownership of 50% or more is what makes an entity blocked; control alone does
not automatically block it, but it is a risk factor handled through this policy’s escalation route. Total
ownership is discoverable only through the SOP-10 ownership structure work; this policy therefore
adopts GOV-01 conclusion C4 as its own: ownership-structure evidence standards are a sanctions
control, and an ownership structure that would not support the 50% computation does not support
onboarding. Control is assessed on the SOP-10 S4.2 criteria. Where an alert is unresolved, the
affected funds and transactions are put on immediate hold and the investigation is urgent; the
formal freeze follows as soon as the legal threshold is met, per SOP-21.


7 · Screening obligations
Who: customers, beneficial owners as identified (not as declared), directors and signatories,
intermediate ownership structure entities, payment counterparties within product area data, and, on
the Crypto Service, wallet addresses per SOP-17. When: at onboarding before activation; ongoing,
with delta-screening on every list update against the full base; at transaction level for payments and
Crypto Service activity; and on any event that changes the persons in a relationship. How: per
SOP-12, with match the decision on hits per C-4; near-match dismissing requires recorded rationale
on mismatched secondary identifiers and is never based on the customer's own assurance.


8 · On a match
A confirmed match triggers SOP-21 without customer notification: freeze of funds and suspension of
services, no execution of pending transactions, reporting to the competent authorities without delay,
and MLRO ownership of everything thereafter. A match that cannot yet be dismissed triggers an
immediate hold on the affected funds and transactions and an urgent investigation, and converts to
a freeze the moment the legal threshold is met. Suspicion arising with the match is assessed under
SOP-20 in parallel. Deciding to "monitor while clarifying" instead of freezing is not an available
option and would be a breach of this policy.


9 · Licences and exemptions
Frozen funds may in some situations lawfully be released or used, but only with a permit from the
competent authority. The typical grounds are set out in the regimes themselves: basic living costs,
legal fees, or payments under contracts that predate the listing. Where such a ground may exist,
Mynta applies to the authority; nothing moves before the permit is in hand and its conditions are met
exactly. Permit handling is MLRO-owned, documented, and reported to the board.

10 · Governance
The MLRO owns this policy's operation: list updates applied without undue delay from the moment
they take legal effect, weekends included: same-day ingestion as the operating standard,
emergency manual controls while an update propagates, blocking on too old lists, and full-base
delta screening after every update. Vendor SLAs support this but do not transfer Mynta’s
responsibility, configuration and tuning of the screening system with test evidence retained, the C-2
category list's sanctions-driven entries, and breach and incident handling with supervisory
notification where required. Screening effectiveness (coverage, match quality, dismiss discipline) is
tested at least annually and after material configuration change, with results in the MLRO report.
Training per GOV-02 section 9 includes sanctions modules for relevant roles.


11 · Records and review
Screening records, decisions on hits, freezes, reports and licence files follow GOV-02 section 8
retention. This policy is reviewed annually and immediately upon material regime change, with the
change history maintained alongside.
