1 · Purpose, scope and legal basis
This document is Mynta AB's business-wide assessment of how its products, services, customers,
distribution channels, geographic exposure and transaction patterns can be used for money
laundering and terrorist financing, and of the risks that follow for the institution. It is prepared under
penningtvättslagen (2017:630) 2 kap 1 § and FFFS 2017:11, having regard to the Swedish national risk
assessment, the Commission's supranational risk assessment and the EBA risk factor guidelines
(EBA/GL/2021/02). It covers Mynta AB in its entirety, including the distribution of crypto services
provided by Mynta Digital AB, whose own CASP obligations are assessed in that entity's parallel
documentation; the exposure Mynta AB carries as distributor and fiat product area is assessed here.

The assessment binds the rest of the FCP framework: GOV-02 to GOV-09 take their risk premises
from it, SOP-14 risk scoring must reconcile customer-level classifications to it, and every procedure's
controls exist to mitigate a risk identified here. Where an operational decision would contradict a
conclusion in section 14, the conclusion prevails until this document is revised through its own
governance. Residual risks are accepted through informed decisions recorded in section 13, within
the risk appetite. Acceptance can cover a business exposure that is effectively managed; it never
covers regulatory non-compliance, insufficient customer due diligence or ineffective controls. Risks
shall not be understated to avoid acceptance decisions.


2 · Methodology

2.1 Structure
Risk is assessed as inherent risk, before controls; controls are then identified per risk area with their
owning documents; residual risk is the rating after controls. The unit of assessment is the risk area
within each dimension (products, customers, geography, channels, transactions), with terrorist
financing and sanctions assessed as parallel dimensions in sections 10 and 11 because their drivers
differ from laundering.

2.3 Rating discipline
Ratings are argued from evidence, not asserted: each area's rating cites the typologies it maps onto,
the canon data that sizes the exposure, and, where available, Mynta's own outcomes (alerts, reports,
exits, confirmed fraud). Adding up within a dimension is worst-case: the dimension carries its highest
residual area. A blended average would obscure the exposure this assessment is required to show.
Cross-dimension interactions (a high-risk product sold through a high-risk channel to a high-risk
segment) are assessed in section 9 and in the section 14 conclusions rather than multiplied
mechanically.

2.4 Data sources and their limits
Quantitative inputs come from the 2025 figures in the master profile and from monitoring, screening
and casework statistics; qualitative inputs from the national and supranational risk assessments,
FATF and Nordic typology reporting, and supervisory communications. Known limits are stated where
they matter: monitoring statistics measure what current rules detect, not what occurs; confirmed-
fraud figures understate attempted fraud; and the Crypto Service's short history (distribution began
2026) makes its internal statistics thin, which the ratings treat as grounds for caution. Because the
national risk assessment 2024-2025 rates both the threat and the vulnerability of the e-money
sector as high, and names the fiat-to-crypto bridge as particularly exposed, a low or medium rating
in this document must rest on documented internal evidence, not on the absence of observed abuse.
The methodology follows the proportionality the AMLA draft guidelines allow smaller institutions, in
a more qualitative form, while recording that Mynta’s customer and volume numbers do not make it a
non-complex institution.

2.5 Governance of this assessment
The MLRO owns the assessment and the analysis; the board approves it and any change to
accepted residual risk. Section 15 sets the annual cycle and event triggers. Every revision states
what changed and why in Appendix D, and material rating changes are traceable to evidence, not to
convenience.

4 · Threat environment
The threat picture relevant to a Swedish EMI of Mynta's shape, drawn from the national and
supranational assessments, FATF typology work and Nordic enforcement experience, in the order
they bear on Mynta:

  • Transaction laundering through merchant acquiring. Miscoded or front merchants processing
    payments for undisclosed activity, the sector's principal typology and the one supervisory action
    most often cites against acquirers. Attractiveness scales with onboarding speed and weak
    website-to-business verification, both of which Mynta's model must control by procedure rather
    than by human volume.
  • Mule networks across retail accounts. Recruited or synthetic account holders receiving and
    forwarding fraud proceeds, increasingly fast under instant payments. The 2025 confirmed-fraud
    count (1,460, mostly APP and card) shows the fraud pressure next door. Mule activity is the
    laundering that follows it.
  • Structuring and smurfing across product areas. Values kept under review thresholds, split across
    accounts, cards and Crypto Service activity. A customer clean on each product area can structure
    across them, which is why section 9 treats cross-product area visibility as a named risk area
    rather than a control detail.
  • Layering through crypto. Rapid conversion and onward movement through exchanges and self-
    hosted wallets, with counterparty opacity that fiat product areas do not present. Mynta's
    exposure is kept within limits by approved listing and volume caps but is structurally the fastest-
    moving channel it touches.
  • Ownership concealment in the business book. False or incomplete UBO declarations, layered
    holdcos, nominee arrangements: the entry-point typology for every downstream abuse, and the
    reason SOP-10's the adding up of ownership and discrepancy machinery carries the weight it
    does.
  • Terrorist financing. Low-value, high-speed flows to risk cross-border flows and crypto off-Crypto
    Service, with weak pre-indicators; assessed in section 10 on its own drivers.

The environment is dynamic: instant payments raise speed economy-wide, AMLR will reset parts of
the control baseline in 2027, and criminal method migrates toward institution classes with weaker
verification. The assessment therefore gives more weight to structural attractiveness than to last
year's incident counts.

5.1 E-money accounts and payments
Exposure. SEK 21.4bn of the 38.2bn processed; the default product for all 159,000-plus customers;
SEPA reach across the EEA.

Vulnerabilities and typologies. Mule receipt and forwarding; structuring under thresholds; rapid in-
and-out flows inconsistent with stated profile; business accounts used as pass-throughs for
undisclosed third-party activity. No cash placement exists anywhere in the product. The classic
placement stage therefore does not exist here, and abuse concentrates in layering and transfers.

Inherent rating: medium. The product offers usable laundering infrastructure with genuine speed and
reach, but without the opacity or intensity of acquiring or crypto; recognised typologies exist and
appear in Mynta's own alert flow at expected rates.

Principal controls. SOP-10/11 onboarding with monitoring baselines per SOP-10 section 16; SOP-18
rules on velocity, profile deviation and pass-through patterns; SOP-12 ongoing screening; SOP-22
event-driven review.

5.2 Card issuing
Exposure. 96,000 active cards; SEK 3.6bn spend; ATM access and cross-border acceptance vithe
scheme.

Vulnerabilities and typologies. Cash extraction through ATM withdrawal as a quasi-placement route;
spend layering across borders; corporate cards drawing on business accounts as a secondary
extraction path; card credentials as the monetisation point of account-takeover fraud.

Inherent rating: medium. The cash-extraction path is the most dangerous part, and it is kept within
limits by limits. Otherwise the product area carries the same risks as accounts, on a narrower
surface.

Principal controls. SOP-16 limits by risk class and ATM caps; SOP-18 card-product area rules
including extraction-pattern detection; issuing activation only per SOP-10/11 outcomes.

5.3 Merchant acquiring
Exposure. SEK 11.9bn through 620 merchants: a third of institutional volume through 0.4% of
customers, e-commerce only.

Vulnerabilities and typologies. Transaction laundering by front or miscoded merchants; undisclosed
the adding up of ownership (a merchant processing for others); sudden-volume merchants with
fabricated trading history; chargeback-pattern abuse; collusive refund schemes; laundering of a
merchant's own criminal proceeds through inflated legitimate-looking sales. The concentration of
value in few counterparties makes each underwriting error material.

Inherent rating: high. This is the sector's principal typology mapped directly onto Mynta's second-
largest product area, It is attractive to abusers for the same properties that make the product
commercial: fast onboarding, remote relationships, and volume that scales.

Principal controls. SOP-15 underwriting with C-7 MCC governance and the SOP-10 Appendix D
checks; chargeback and refund monitoring per SOP-18's acquiring rules; periodic review at the
section-appropriate intervals; GOV-04 prohibitions with no exception path.

5.4 Crypto exposure via Mynta Digital AB
Exposure. SEK 1.3bn Crypto Service volume; 18,900 crypto-active consumers and 41 business
customers; distribution began 2026, so internal statistics are thin.

Vulnerabilities and typologies. Layering speed and finality; counterparty opacity beyond the first
hop; off-Crypto Service use of proceeds acquired elsewhere; sanctioned-wallet exposure; and the
structural pattern of fiat product areas being used to cash out abuse that happened entirely on-
ownership structure. This puts Mynta at the cash-out point, without visibility of what happened
upstream.

Inherent rating: very high. The product area combines the highest speed, the widest reach and the
least counterparty transparency of anything Mynta touches. Criminal migration toward crypto off-
Crypto Service is the clearest current trend in the threat picture. The thin internal history is treated
as grounds for caution.

Principal controls. C-8 approved listing with GOV-09 gate for additions; SOP-17 wallet screening,
travel-rule data handling and VASP counterparty due diligence; mandatory EDD per C-9 above the
30% activity trigger; GOV-04 volume caps; the section 13 requirement that this product area is
operated only while limited to high, with the limits re-tested at every review and the activity
restricted or suspended on a breach.

7 · Geographic risk
Direct footprint. 68% Sweden, 29% rest-of-EEA, 3% non-EEA by customer count; SEK and EUR
dominant settlement. On its own this is a low-to-medium exposure: strong-registry jurisdictions, EU
supervisory baseline, no branch or agent presence anywhere.

Indirect exposure, where the real risk sits. Merchant ownership chains touch roughly 40
jurisdictions, including registry-weak and secrecy-favourable ones; crypto counterparties are
without geographic limits by construction; and payment cross-border flows reach wherever SEPA
and card product areas reach. The geographic risk of a Stockholm-registered merchant can therefore
be materially the high-risk category while formally the standard category. That is why connection
(SOP-10 section 15) is defined through the ownership structure and the beneficial owners, not
through the registration line alone.

How it works in practice. Rule C-2 categories govern: the standard category standard handling; the
elevated category heightened scrutiny and confirmation by a second source; the high-risk category
mandatory EDD and MLRO decision; prohibited jurisdictions decline at any layer, whenever
discovered. The category list is maintained under GOV-03 and C-2 from the EU high-risk-third-
country list, FATF listings and Mynta's own additions, reviewed at least quarterly.




Mynta AB | GOV-01 | v2.0                                                                         Page 8 of 17
Inherent rating: high, carried by the indirect exposure; the direct footprint alone would rate medium.
Principal controls: C-2 tiering, SOP-10 ownership-structure evidence standards, SOP-13 EDD on the
high-risk category connection, GOV-03 screening at transaction and relationship level.


8 · Distribution and channel risk
Mynta has exactly one distribution channel: fully remote digital onboarding, direct, with no branches,
no agents, no intermediated distribution and no face-to-face fallback. Removed risks: agent
misconduct, intermediary KYC failure, and the classic distribution-ownership structure opacity the
EBA guidelines flag. Concentrated risks: impersonation and synthetic identity at scale, document
forgery against remote verification, and account-opening automation abuse (scripted applications
probing the funnel).

Verification strength differs by flow, by design: BankID for Swedish residents is a high-assurance
factor covering the large majority of consumer onboarding; document-plus-liveness for non-
Swedish applicants is materially weaker against determined forgery, and the assessment treats the
non-BankID flow as the channel's weakest point, mitigated by SOP-11's escalation of repeated
verification failure and SOP-18's new-account rules. The crypto Crypto Service adds no separate
channel: access is a product flag on an already-onboarded relationship (SOP-10 Appendix E).

Inherent rating: high (non-face-to-face at full scale is a listed enhanced-risk factor and the fraud
pressure is real: 1,460 confirmed cases in 2025). Residual: medium, resting on e-ID strength in the
dominant flow, layered verification standards in SOP-10/11 section 7, and monitoring designed around
account age.


9 · Transaction risk, including cross-product area
Transaction risk is assessed as its own dimension because Mynta's four product areas multiply
paths: value can enter on one product area, move on a second and exit on a third, and each product
area's rules, viewed alone, can read the pattern as normal. Named risk areas:

  • Velocity and pass-through. Rapid in-and-out flows inconsistent with the S3 baseline; the
    instant-payments environment shortens reaction time economy-wide and raises the value of pre-
    set rules over manual review.
  • Structuring. Values held under review thresholds, split across accounts, cards and Crypto
    Service; twin behaviour across related customers.
  • Cross-product area structuring. The principal exposure of Mynta's shape: card loads feeding
    Crypto Service purchases, acquiring settlements funding onward transfers, each leg
    unremarkable per product area. The control requirement is architectural: SOP-18 must total a
    customer's activity across every product area in one view, and rules must run on the total; per-
    product area-only monitoring is treated in this assessment as a control failure, not a design
    choice.
  • Baseline deviation. Activity departing from the recorded S3 profile in volume, geography,
    counterparty type or crypto share; the SOP-10 section 16 handover exists so this comparison is
    always possible.

Inherent rating: high. Residual: high, accepted per section 13: monitoring limits and detects this
exposure, but cannot remove what four fast product areas create, and section 13 accepts that
plainly. Principal controls: SOP-18 cross-product arethe adding up of ownership and rule sets per

10 · Terrorist financing
Assessed separately because the drivers are different: values are small, speed matters more than
concealment, and warning signs before the fact are weak. Detection therefore leans on screening,
cross-border flows and behavioural anomaly, not on value thresholds.

Exposure by product. Accounts and cards: rapid low-value transfers and spend in or toward risk
cross-border flows; the card product area's cross-border acceptance is the most exposed part.
Acquiring: low relevance, with the residual scenario being a front merchant generating funds. Crypto
Crypto Service: the highest-attention path, off-ramping or acquiring value with speed and reach;
approved listing limits but does not remove it. Mynta's NPO segment is negligible in count and is
handled at onboarding rather than carrying a standing segment assessment.

Controls. GOV-03 list screening at customer and transaction level with no value-threshold
exemptions; SOP-18 cross-border flow rules maintained against the C-2 categories and current
threat reporting; SOP-17 wallet screening on the Crypto Service; SOP-20 recognising TF indicators
as reportable in their own right, with the same tipping prohibition.

Inherent rating: medium (Mynta's profile offers usable but not preferred TF infrastructure; no cash,
no informal-value channels, thin cross-border flow presence). Residual: medium, with one caveat
stated openly: a TF detection failure is rare but severe. Section 15 reflects this by making relevant
external events an out-of-cycle review trigger.


11 · Sanctions risk
Assessed as its own dimension because the obligation is absolute and strict-liability in character: a
single missed true match is a breach regardless of intent, and the 2025 experience (2 true matches,
both frozen and reported per SOP-21) shows the exposure is present in the book.

Exposure paths. Direct: a listed person as customer, signatory or beneficial owner. Indirect, the
harder problem: ownership and control by listed persons under the 50% rule, reachable only through
the SOP-10 ownership structure work, since a clean front with a sanctioned total owner passes
name screening alone. Transactional: counterparties of payments and, on the Crypto Service, wallet
exposure to listed or blocked addresses. Circumvention: the post-2022 environment's
professionalised evasion through layered structures and third countries, This raises the value of the
elevated category confirmation by a second source, and lowers the weight given to chains that are
documented but not confirmed by a second source.

List regime. EU and UN lists are binding baseline; the OFAC position is risk-based per GOV-03:
limited direct USD business narrows the connection, but scheme, sponsor and correspondent
expectations mean OFAC cannot simply be ignored. Designated OFAC programmes are therefore
screened with the decision on hits per C-4 and the stance is documented in GOV-03 rather than
improvised per case.

Inherent rating: high (environment-driven). Residual: medium: screening coverage on identified
persons per SOP-10 S5.1's identified-not-declared rule, C-4 the decision on hits discipline, immediate
SOP-21 freeze-and-report, and ownership-structure evidence standards that make 50%-rule



Mynta AB | GOV-01 | v2.0                                                                      Page 10 of 17
exposure discoverable. The residual acceptance notes its dependency: it holds only while S4
ownership structure quality holds, which section 14 turns into a binding conclusion.

14 · Conclusions binding the framework
These conclusions bind every procedure and every individual decision until this document is revised
through section 15. Contradicting them operationally is a deviation requiring the GOV-02 incident
treatment.

  • C1. Complex structures and any high-risk-category connection always route through SOP-13 EDD
    and are decided at MLRO level or, where the governance framework so designates, by a risk
    committee; no score, workload or commercial reason overrides the route.
  • C2. Acquiring underwriting standards admit no deviation: SOP-15 and C-7, with the underwriting
    requirements and the prohibited list clearly defined, apply in full and consistently to every
    merchant, and the prohibited list has no exception path at any level.
  • C3. Crypto exposure operates only inside its limits: C-8 approved list, GOV-04 caps, mandatory
    EDD above the C-9 trigger; a limit breached restricts or suspends the affected activity until
    restored; growth does not resume before that.
  • C4. Sanctions residual is conditional on the quality of ownership and control data: S4 evidence
    standards are a sanctions control, not only a KYB control, and degrade together. A material
    weakness triggers correction and reassessment of the residual rating.
  • C5. Monitoring assesses activity across product areas as a whole; product area-specific
    monitoring is acceptable only where the interactions between product areas are also assessed.
    Activation without the section 16 baseline is prohibited.
  • C6. Screening runs on the defined population: the customer, beneficial owners identified under
    SOP-10 S4, representatives, directors and the other persons the routines designate. Identified
    persons, not declared ones, at onboarding and ongoing.
  • C7. Combinations of risk factors are assessed jointly, not by inheriting the highest individual
    rating. A relationship whose joint residual lands at very high is onboarded or retained only where
    the section 13 limiting requirements are met; categorical prohibitions follow GOV-04 and C-7, not
    this scale.
  • C8. Risk classifications reconcile to this document; overrides are MLRO-approved and registered,
    and systematic override pressure triggers reassessment of this document, not quiet drift in the
    scoring model.
  • C9. The data limits in 2.4 are a standing constraint: monitoring statistics are treated as partial
    evidence, and typology change routes through SOP-25 into rules and, where material, into this
    assessment out of cycle.

15 · Governance, review and event triggers
Cycle. Annual review owned by the MLRO: data refresh from the canon sources, rating re-argument,
controls-evidence check against section 12, board approval of the result. The review calendar places
data cut-off, drafting, challenge and approval so the approved version is never older than 15 months
at any point.

Event triggers for out-of-cycle review, in whole or per section: a new product, product area, token or
cross-border flow (GOV-09); a supervisory finding or communicated expectation; a material change
in the national or supranational assessment, the EU high-risk list or FATF listings; a sanctions-regime
shift material to the book; an internally observed typology not reflected here; a control found
inoperative in QC or audit; and the AMLR application date, which triggers a full re-baselining through
SOP-25 ahead of July 2027.

Versioning and dissemination. Versions and changes per Appendix D; superseded versions retained.
The approved document is read-and-attested by the compliance function and the accountable
product owners; procedures citing it reference the version they were aligned to, and SOP-25 tracks
realignment when this document moves.
