1 · Purpose and principle
Enhanced due diligence deepens the understanding of a relationship where the risk concentrates:
more evidence, stronger confirmation by a second source, senior decisions, tighter monitoring. The
principle is proportional depth: the measures fit the trigger. A the high-risk category connection calls
for geographic and counterparty depth. A PEP calls for wealth-and-funds depth. There is no single
maximal checklist applied to everything. The report shall state whether the risk is acceptable, kept
within limits and monitorable, on what basis, or conclude that it is not.


2 · Triggers
Mandatory, per C-9 and law: any PEP connection; any high-risk-category or EU high-risk third-
country connection; ownership chains of three or more layers or any legal-arrangement layer;
expected or actual crypto share at or above 30%; confirmed designated adverse media. By referral:
SOP-19 investigation outcomes that keep the relationship, SOP-22 event findings, GOV-04 restricted
categories that condition holding on EDD (licensed PSPs and MSBs, crypto-sector businesses,
NPOs), and any file a decision-maker chooses to route here, with the reason recorded. Where several
triggers apply at once, the scope covers the measures for all of them, without doing the same
measure twice.

4 · Procedure
S1         Intake: the referral states its triggers and attaches the file. The EDD owner (a senior analyst)
           confirms that the triggers are read correctly, and logs the case with target dates aligned to
           C-5.

S2         Scoping: select the measures from section 3 for every trigger present, and record them as
           the EDD plan. If a measure is deliberately left out, write down that it was left out, and why.

S3         Collection: evidence to section 5 standards; requests to the customer are consolidated
           where possible and logged; IF the customer cannot or will not evidence a scoped item,
           THEN the report says so plainly and treats it as a finding. Scoped items shall not be omitted
           from the report.

S4         Analysis: the report (T-3) answers the scoped questions, states what was confirmed by a
           second source versus taken on documentation alone, and concludes with a risk view and
           recommended conditions (caps, monitoring profile, review cycle). The T-3 report covers the
           triggers, the scope, the evidence, source of funds and wealth, red flags, unresolved issues,
           the risk conclusion, any conditions, the monitoring set, and the approvals.

5 · Evidence standards for source of funds and wealth
Source of funds explains the money in the relationship; source of wealth explains how the person
came to have wealth at all. Both are documented on a hierarchy: independent and verifiable first
(audited accounts, registry filings, completed-transaction documents, tax records), institutional
second (bank statements, employer confirmations), self-produced last and never alone (declarations,
CVs). Evidence is selected for the fact it must prove; the order above states preference, not
exclusion. Confirmation by a second source means two independent sources agreeing on the
material fact. Reasonable is assessed against scale: the wealth shall be explainable by something
documented, such as a career, a business history or completed transactions. A thick pile of
documents does not replace that explanation. The report states which level of the hierarchy each
material fact rests on.


6 · Timelines and quality
EDD suspends the referring routine's decision clock; target completion is 15 business days from
complete referral for single-trigger cases and 25 for stacked triggers, with delays logged and visible
in the MLRO report. Quality: 100% second review before decision per the SOP-10 §13 second review
standard; EDD reports are drawn at random in the monthly QC alongside onboarding files, graded on
the same scale.
