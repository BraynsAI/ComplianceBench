1 · Purpose
The framework shall be kept current with regulation, deliberately and on record. This instruction sets
the loop: capture what changed, decide whether it touches Mynta, find the gaps precisely, correct
with owners and dates, and verify the change actually happened. Quality is judged against two
failure modes. A missed gap leaves the institution non-compliant. A false gap consumes change
capacity for nothing. The relevance and gap analyses are therefore held to the same evidence
discipline as casework.


2 · Sources and cadence
Monitored sources include the EU Official Journal, EBA and ESMA guidelines and Q&As,
Finansinspektionen’s regulations and communications, FATF publications, and the industry guidance
the function designates. The source list is reviewed at least annually.

3 · Procedure
S1        Capture. Each item enters the regulatory change register with: source, instrument and
          provision, publication date, entry-into-force and application dates, and a one-line subject.
          Transitional structures (a regulation in force but applying later, an RTS pending under a
          level-1 text) are captured with both dates, because the gap analysis in S3 depends on
          knowing which regime governs when.

S2        Relevance first sorting. In scope or not, with a recorded reason either way. The test is
          Mynta's activities and perimeter, not the instrument's headline: a banking-specific rule can
          still touch the safeguarding bank relationship, and a headline AML change can be out of
          scope where it addresses activities Mynta does not perform. Out-of-scope calls are drawn at
          random in the annual look-back because first sorting errors are the least visible failure point.

S3        Gap analysis. For in-scope items, map the provision against the framework through the
          GOV-02 §15 implementation map and then the affected documents themselves, clause by
          clause where the provision is specific. Each finding is classified: compliant (covered), partly
          compliant (covered with gaps), missing (obligation with no implementing text), conflicting
          (framework says otherwise), not applicable (documented, with the reason), future
          requirement (not yet applying, tracked to its date), or superseded (framework implements
          the old regime), or compliant (covered, with the covering reference cited). The analysis
          records the legal-hierarchy reading where sources stack: what is directly applicable, what is
          binding national implementation, what is comply-or-explain, and which provision governs
          during transition. A gap list without the compliant findings shall not be accepted; coverage
          shall be documented, not assumed.

S4        Correction plan. Per gap: the change (document text, control, system configuration,
          training), the owner from the document-ownership register, the date, and any interim
          measure where the application date arrives before the fix. Interim measures are themselves
          logged as deviations where they fall short of a live obligation.

S5        Approval. Policy-category and appetite changes route to the board; routine-category to the
          MLRO; GOV-01 impacts trigger its §15 event review. Approvals reference the register item so
          the record links the legal source to the implemented change.

S6        Implement and verify. The owner delivers; the compliance function verifies the delivered
          text or control against the gap it closes, and only verification closes the register item.
          Training content is updated where the change touches operating roles.

4 · The AMLR programme
Regulation (EU) 2024/1624 applies from 10 July 2027 and is run as a standing workstream, not as
individual sweep items. The workstream maintains four things: an article-by-article inventory
mapped to the framework; the tracked RTS and guideline dependencies, each with its own dates; a
re-baselining of GOV-01 ahead of application, per its trigger; and a transition calendar, so that
documents citing penningtvättslagen provisions that the AMLR displaces are re-anchored before the
date, not after it. Progress is a standing item in the quarterly MLRO report until closure.


5 · Register and reporting
The regulatory change register carries every item from capture to verified closure with its
classification history. Quarterly reporting to the board vithe MLRO report: new in-scope items, open
gaps by age and severity, overdue corrections by owner, and AMLR programme status. An open gap
past its application date is reported as a compliance incident under GOV-02 §14, whatever the
correction status.


6 · Quality and review
Annual look-back: sample of out-of-scope first sorting calls re-reviewed; closed gaps re-verified on
a sample; timeliness measured from publication to first sorting and from application date to closure.
This instruction is reviewed annually and immediately when the sources themselves change shape,
as they will when AMLA guidance begins issuing.
