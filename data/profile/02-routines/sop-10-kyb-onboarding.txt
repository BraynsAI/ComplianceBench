1 · Purpose and scope
Know Your Business ("KYB") means the checks Mynta AB ("Mynta" or "the company") performs on
companies and other legal persons before they become customers. This work procedure describes
those checks step by step. It implements the customer due diligence requirements in the AML/CTF
policy (GOV-02) and the risk decisions in the business-wide risk assessment (GOV-01) and the risk
appetite (GOV-04).

It applies to every legal person that applies for any Mynta product. This includes merchants applying
for acquiring and business customers requesting the Crypto Service through Mynta Digital AB. It
does not cover private individuals (SOP-11), ongoing due diligence (SOP-22) or termination of
business relationships (SOP-23).

All steps in section 6 are mandatory unless a step states its own trigger. No product may be
activated before the section 6 procedure is finished and the file is approved at the correct level.

5.1 Guidance per entity type
Swedish AB. The registreringsbevis proves that the company exists and who signs for it. It says
nothing about ownership. The ownership evidence is the aktiebok, and the customer produces it.
Because of that, the analyst shall check it against the beneficial-ownership register entry. Where the
ownership structure continues upward, the analyst shall also obtain registry evidence for each
holding company. Watch for one pattern: a newly formed AB (under 12 months) that expects
immediate high volumes. That is not reasonable on its face, and S3.2 applies.

Handelsbolag and kommanditbolag. Partners are jointly liable. Each general partner is treated as
having control, regardless of profit share, unless the bolagsavtal shows otherwise. The
kommanditdelägare’s capital share still counts toward the 25% calculation.

Ekonomisk förening. Ownership rarely concentrates in a förening, but control can. Assess the voting
rules in the stadgar, any rights to appoint board members, and any dominant member. The senior-
managing-official option is common here. That is correct when the S4.3 conditions are met.

Stiftelse. A foundation has no owners, so beneficial ownership follows control and benefit. Look at
board members or administrators with decisive influence, founders who kept rights for themselves,
and identifiable groups of beneficiaries. Foundations applying for acquiring are rare. When it
happens, the file goes to SOP-13 on the complex-structure trigger.

EEA companies. Registry quality varies between countries. In many of them the national register
does not show shareholdings. In that case the share register comes from the customer, and it must
be checked one level further up against registry evidence. National BO registers are used where they
exist. They are filings, not proof, exactly as in Sweden.

Non-EEA companies. The legalisation matters as much as the document itself. An apostilled certified
copy of a share register proves the copy as at its issue date, so the C-1 age rule applies to that issue
date. Countries in the C-2 high-risk category always add the SOP-13 route.

Listed companies. The simplification covers the ownership evidence only. It applies only while the
listing is verified as current, on a regulated market with adequate disclosure requirements. An MTF
listing does not qualify. For a majority-owned subsidiary, the majority link must be documented.
Group membership stated by the customer is not enough.

per GOV-04. As account customers they require MLRO pre-approval before S1 begins. The trustee,

6 · Procedure

S1 · Intake and completeness
S1.1 Receive the application. It must contain: the onboarding form; the UBO declaration (template
T-1); an ownership chart drawn by the customer; a registry extract for the applicant meeting C-1; the
share register or equivalent per section 5; identity documents for the signatories; a description of
the business and the purpose of the relationship; expected products, volumes and counterparty
countries; and, for acquiring, the MCC, the transacting website and any processing history.

S1.2 Check completeness against Appendix A within 2 business days of receipt. IF any item is
missing, unreadable or too old under C-1, THEN request the item, record the request, and pause the
assessment clock. Do not begin the assessment on an incomplete file. If quality control finds
conclusions drawn on an incomplete file, that is recorded as a finding.

S1.3 Check the applicant’s stated business against the GOV-04 prohibited industries and structures.
For acquiring, also check the C-7 list. IF the business is prohibited, THEN decline per S8.3. No one at
any level may make an exception (C-6).

S1.4 Record the target decision date: 10 business days from a complete file for standard cases.
Where EDD applies, the SOP-13 timelines apply instead.

S2 · Identification and verification of the customer
S2.1 Verify that the customer exists: exact registered name, legal form, registered address and
registration number, against the official registry for the country, using the section 5 table. The extract
must meet the C-1 age limit. An older extract counts as missing.

S2.2 Identify the natural persons acting for the customer. Verify their identity per section 7. Verify
their authority against the extract, or against a power of attorney meeting section 7. IF authority
cannot be established from registry evidence or a valid power of attorney, THEN the file is
incomplete. Return to S1.2.

S2.3 Record any trading names, group membership and licences relevant to the stated business.
Example: gambling or MSB licences, where C-7 makes permission conditional on a licence.

S2.4 Establish the actual place of business where it differs from the registered address. Virtual-
office registrations are common and legitimate, but the operating address feeds the risk rating and,
for acquiring, the website checks in Appendix D. IF no operating presence can be established
anywhere, THEN record this as a C-9 complex-structure indicator.

S2.5 Record the extract references and dates, the verification outcome for each person, and any
authority documents relied on. For S2 as a whole: existence, identity and authority must each rest on
evidence meeting section 7.

S3 · Purpose and nature of the relationship
S3.1 Record the business model, the reason for seeking the relationship, the expected products,
expected monthly volumes and average transaction sizes per product area, counterparty countries,


Mynta AB | SOP-10 | v2.0 sample, plain register                                                  Page 5 of 17
and settlement currencies. For acquiring: open the website and verify that it exists, that it sells what
the application states, and that it matches the declared MCC. The profile recorded here becomes the
baseline that transaction monitoring (SOP-18) works against.

S3.2 IF the stated profile is not reasonable against the company’s size, age, sector or evidence (for
example, expected volumes with no trading history behind them), THEN treat this as a discrepancy.
Document it and apply S4.5 in the same way: escalate. Do not resolve it by assumption.

S3.3 Record the expected counterparties by type and country: payers, payees, marketplaces, and
any PSP relationships elsewhere. For the Crypto Service, record the expected split between crypto
activity and account activity. These fields are mandatory even when the customer’s answer is "none
yet". An empty expectation is still a monitoring baseline.

S4 · Beneficial ownership
S4.1 Obtain the customer’s UBO declaration (T-1) and the customer’s entry in the beneficial-
ownership register. Treat both with the same caution. The declaration is a claim to be verified. The
register entry is the customer’s own filing, so it is verified too, not relied on.

S4.2 Identify every natural person whose direct and indirect holdings add up to more than 25% of
ownership or control. The calculation crosses every level: for each intermediate company, attribute
its holding to that company’s owners in proportion, and add up per natural person across all paths.
Control in other ways also counts toward beneficial ownership regardless of shareholding: voting
agreements, the right to appoint or remove a majority of the board, or comparable arrangements. IF
the customer is high risk, THEN document the ownership structure from 10% instead, per rule C-3.

S4.3 The senior-managing-official option may be recorded as beneficial owner only when two things
are both true: the ownership structure is fully mapped with registry evidence meeting C-1 at every
level, and no natural person meets the S4.2 test. An unmapped structure never qualifies. A structure
supported only by too-old documents, or only by documents the customer produced, never qualifies
either.

S4.4 Verify the identity of each beneficial owner identified in S4.2, with identity evidence per section
7. Verify every ownership link with registry evidence per C-1 and section 5. A chart drawn by the
customer is never enough on its own. It must be checked against registry evidence.

S4.5 IF the declaration, the register entry and the registry evidence disagree on any ownership fact,
THEN stop. Do not proceed, and do not decline. Escalate to a senior analyst per SOP-19 with a
discrepancy memo per template T-2. The memo contains: the recalculated ownership structure with
sources, the specific discrepancy, and the requests needed to resolve it. Where the recalculated,
documented ownership contradicts the register entry, report this to Bolagsverket without delay
under 3 kap. 5 § lagen (2017:631) om registrering av verkliga huvudmän. Do not wait for the
customer’s response. Record the report and its date in the memo. The customer is told only that
additional review is ongoing (S8.2).

S4.6 Document the final ownership structure: every natural person over the threshold, every
intermediate company with its country and percentage per path, the evidence for each link, and the
calculation. The standard is simple: a reviewer must be able to repeat the arithmetic without asking
the analyst anything.




Mynta AB | SOP-10 | v2.0 sample, plain register                                                Page 6 of 17
S4 guidance · Calculating total ownership
The method is proportional calculation through the ownership structure. For each intermediate
company, multiply the customer’s holding in it by each owner’s holding in the intermediate. Do this
level by level, and add up per natural person across all paths.

Mynta does not count an intermediate’s holding in full once a person controls that intermediate, for
the 25% arithmetic. But control in other ways is assessed separately under S4.2, and it can establish
beneficial ownership on its own. Where the arithmetic lands a person just under 25% while that
person plainly controls the structure, the control test decides, and the file documents why.

Worked example 1, one indirect path. Customer: 24% held directly by person A; 32% held by Holdco
H; H is 78% owned by A. A’s total: 24% + (32% × 78%) = 24% + 24.96% = 48.96%. A is a beneficial
owner. A declaration naming no beneficial owner is false, and S4.5 applies.

Worked example 2, several paths. Customer: 15% direct by person B; 40% by Holdco X, where B
owns 60% of X; 20% by Holdco Y, where B owns 30% of Y. B’s total: 15% + (40% × 60%) + (20% ×
30%) = 15% + 24% + 6% = 45%. Every path counts, however small its contribution. A path may be left
uncalculated only when it mathematically cannot reach a contribution that matters, and that is
documented.

Worked example 3, the official option correctly used. Customer: four unrelated holders at 24% each,
plus 26% held by Holdco Z, owned equally by three unrelated persons (8.67% each). The highest total
is 24%. No person meets the threshold, and no control arrangement exists. The structure is fully
mapped with C-1 evidence at every level. So S4.3 permits recording the senior managing official, with
the calculation filed.

Recording standard. File the calculation as a table: person, path, arithmetic, total, and the evidence
reference per link. Round to two decimals. Thresholds are strict: "more than 25%". Where voting
rights differ from capital, calculate a parallel table on voting rights, and the higher result governs.

Common failures found in quality control. Stopping at the first registry level. Accepting the
customer’s chart as the ownership structure. Calculating only the largest path. Treating a too-old
foreign extract as good enough because the Swedish extract is fresh. Recording the official option
while the structure is still unmapped, "pending documents". Each of these is recorded as a section 13
finding.

S5 · Screening
S5.1 Screen per SOP-12: the customer, every beneficial owner identified under S4, every signatory
and director, and every intermediate company in the ownership structure. Screening runs on
identified persons, not declared ones. A person identified by the S4 analysis is screened even if the
customer never declared them. Screening beyond the required population is personal-data
processing without an automatic legal ground: it requires control indications or an approved
deviation, and is never run just to be safe.

S5.2 Decide on matches per SOP-12 and C-4. A near-match may be dismissed at analyst level when
the identifier evidence is strong enough: one strong mismatch, such as date of birth against a
verified document, may be sufficient, while two weak mismatches may not be. The assessment is
recorded. IF a sanctions match is confirmed, THEN SOP-21 applies immediately, this procedure is
suspended, and the customer is not notified.




Mynta AB | SOP-10 | v2.0 sample, plain register                                                 Page 7 of 17
S5.3 Run adverse-media screening on the customer and the identified UBOs per SOP-12. Findings in
the C-9 designated categories trigger SOP-13.

S5.4 File the screening evidence per person: the lists screened, the date, the vendor reference, every
hit with its decision and reason, and any screenings not yet performed marked as pending. The
standard: the screening status of every identified person must be readable in one place. A file that
fails this cannot meet the S7.2 documentation standard.

S6 · Risk rating
S6.1 Rate the customer per SOP-14. The rating must agree with GOV-01. A factor rated high in
GOV-01 cannot produce a low customer rating here, unless the MLRO approves a documented
override and it is logged in the deviation register.

S6.2 IF any C-9 trigger is present, THEN go to SOP-13 before any decision. The C-9 triggers are: any
PEP; a connection to a high-risk country per C-2; an ownership structure of three or more levels, or
any foreign legal-arrangement level; expected crypto turnover above 30% of volume; and
designated adverse media. EDD depth follows the risk rating. Section 8 applies to PEPs.

S6.3 Record the rating inputs and the arithmetic per SOP-14, so the score can be repeated: the
factor values, the weights applied, the resulting rating, and any override with its MLRO approval
reference. A rating stated without its inputs is recorded by quality control as a finding.

S7 · Decision
S7.1 Decide: approve, decline, or escalate, at the level set by C-6. Two limits apply. A file with an
unresolved S4.5 discrepancy or an incomplete S4 ownership structure cannot be approved at any
level. The same file cannot be declined below senior-analyst level. Unresolved ownership is
escalated, not declined, because a decline on a false declaration may itself require SOP-20
assessment.

S7.2 Record the decision reasons so that a reviewer can follow them from the file alone: the facts
relied on, their sources, how each open item was resolved, and the rating arithmetic. Plain text is
acceptable. Conclusions without references are not.

S7.3 Communication standards. Approvals state which products are activated and which information
duties the customer has accepted. Declines follow S8.3. Escalations follow S8.2. No decision
communication ever mentions screening outcomes, suspicion, reporting, or the internal steps of this
work procedure.

S8 · Outcomes and handover
S8.1 On approval: activate only the products applied for. Set the monitoring baseline from S3.1.
Schedule the periodic review per SOP-22 by risk rating: high 12 months, medium 24, low 36. File the
record per section 11.

S8.2 On escalation: SOP-19 owns the case until it is resolved. The customer is told only that review is
ongoing. Nothing that could reveal suspicion is shared, per SOP-20 and meddelandeförbudet.

S8.3 On decline: communicate the decision without revealing screening results, suspicion or internal
assessments. Assess whether the attempted relationship must be reported per SOP-20. Record the
decline and its grounds. Add the entity to the internal declined register, which is checked at S1.

7 · Verification standards

7.1 Natural persons
Swedish residents: BankID. Everyone else: passport or EEA national ID card, through the identity
vendor’s document-and-liveness flow. Where remote verification fails twice, a certified copy of the
identity document per 7.3 is required instead. Representatives, signatories and UBOs are all verified
to this standard. There is no lighter standard for "contact persons" who in fact act for the customer.

7.2 Registry and corporate documents
Swedish documents: electronic extracts directly from Bolagsverket are preferred, and accepted
without certification. Foreign documents: official registry extracts in Swedish, English, Danish or
Norwegian are accepted as issued. Other languages require a certified translation of the operative
parts. Non-EEA documents issued as paper certified copies must be legalised: apostille, or consular
legalisation where the issuing country is outside the Hague Rule.

7.3 Certified copies and powers of attorney
Legalisation of non-EEA documents is applied risk-based: required where document risk or the
issuing country’s risk category warrants it, and always for high-risk-category countries; the analyst
records the assessment either way. Certification is accepted from a notary public, a licensed
attorney, an EEA-licensed credit institution, or a Swedish embassy or consulate. The certifier’s name,
capacity and date must appear on the document. A power of attorney must be specific to the
relationship, or clearly cover it. It must be signed by persons whose own authority is documented.
For non-EEA issuers it must be legalised per 7.2.

7.4 Documentation standards
Every evidence item is filed with its source, its retrieval or issue date, and the analyst’s note of what
it proves. Screenshots of registries are acceptable only as working copies, and only where the
registry provides no export. The URL and timestamp must be captured.

7.6 Document authenticity checks
For customer-supplied documents the analyst checks four things, and records having checked
them: dates are internally consistent; names and numbers agree with the registry evidence; the
document looks intact (no mismatched fonts, misaligned stamps or edited fields); and the issuer can
be verified (registry reference numbers resolve, the certifier is identifiable per 7.3). Suspected
manipulation is never handled by asking the customer for a better copy first. The document is
preserved, the file escalates, and SOP-20 relevance is assessed.


8 · PEPs within KYB
The positions in scope follow the AML Act: heads of state and government; ministers and deputy
ministers; parliamentarians; members of the governing bodies of political parties; supreme and
constitutional court judges; senior officials of auditing authorities and central banks; ambassadors
and chargés d’affaires; senior armed-forces officers; and members of the administrative,
management or supervisory bodies of state-owned enterprises. At international organisations:
directors, deputy directors and board members. Family members are: spouse or partner, children and
their partners, and parents. Known close associates are: a person with joint beneficial ownership
together with a PEP, and a person who is sole beneficial owner of an entity known to exist in practice
for a PEP’s benefit.

Where any beneficial owner, signatory or director is a PEP, a family member of a PEP, or a known
close associate, four things follow. The relationship is high risk, without further assessment. SOP-13
source-of-wealth and source-of-funds measures apply to the PEP’s interest in the customer.
Approval is given by the authorised senior decision-maker the governance framework designates.
Enhanced ongoing monitoring is set per SOP-22. A person who stopped being a PEP within the last
18 months is still treated as a PEP, and longer where the relationship remains high risk. PEP status


Mynta AB | SOP-10 | v2.0 sample, plain register                                                          Page 10 of 17
discovered after onboarding is handled under the SOP-22 event triggers, not under this work
procedure.

The process. PEP status is established in two ways: through SOP-12 screening at S5, and through
the direct question on T-1. A "no" on T-1 combined with a screening hit is a discrepancy, handled the
S4.5 way. On confirmation: the file becomes high risk immediately. SOP-13 opens, with source-of-
wealth and source-of-funds measures in proportion to the PEP’s interest. The evidence must be
documents, not a story: employment and mandate history, disclosed asset registers where public,
and sale or inheritance documents for the capital in question. The decision is recorded in Kompass
before anything is communicated. Monitoring is set to the enhanced profile in SOP-22. Where the
PEP connection sits on a minority owner, below every threshold and without control, the analyst
documents why that person does not drive the relationship’s risk. The decision requirement still
applies. Removing PEP treatment after the 18-month period is an SOP-22 event. It is assessed. It is
never automatic.


9 · Simplified measures
Simplified due diligence may be applied only where GOV-01 supports low risk, and only for two
customer types: a company listed on a regulated market with adequate disclosure requirements, or
its majority-owned subsidiary documented as such; and a Swedish public body. Simplification means
two things: reduced ownership evidence per the section 5 table, and standard rather than enhanced
verification depth. It never means skipping screening (S5). It never means skipping the purpose
assessment (S3). It never means skipping the risk rating (S6). Simplified files remain in the section 13
quality control selection.

Qualification is verified, not taken on trust. The listing is checked against the exchange’s own
register, and the evidence is filed. For non-EEA markets, the analyst assesses whether the market is
regulated and the disclosure rules adequate, and records the assessment. Majority ownership by the
listed parent is documented by the parent’s own disclosures or by registry evidence, and refreshed
at periodic review. Simplification is withdrawn, and the file re-run at standard depth, in four
situations: delisting; loss of the majority link; any C-9 trigger arising; and monitoring behaviour
departing from the profile. SDD files carry the same S3 baseline and the same section 16 handover.
The monitoring system does not know the file was simplified.


10 · Reliance and outsourcing
Mynta does not rely on third parties for the measures in this work procedure, in the sense of the
AML Act’s reliance provisions. The identity and screening vendors are outsourced tools under
GOV-08, and the responsibility stays with Mynta. Information from an introducing partner may inform
S3. It never replaces any step in section 6.


11 · Records and retention
The complete onboarding file is kept for five years after the business relationship ends. This covers
the application, evidence, screening results, ownership documentation, memos, decisions and
communications. The period extends to ten years where law requires it or a competent authority
orders it. Records must be retrievable within the times set in GOV-07, and structured so that




Mynta AB | SOP-10 | v2.0 sample, plain register                                                Page 11 of 17
compliance with each section 6 step can be shown item by item. Declined and abandoned
applications are kept on the same basis.

The file is structured in Kompass in six parts: application and correspondence; evidence, indexed by
section 6 step; screening, per person; ownership documentation and calculation; memos and
assessments; decision and communications. Retrieval standard: the full file within 2 business days
of a competent-authority request, any single evidence item the same day. Formats: original digital
where received digitally, captures per 7.4 otherwise. Nothing is kept only in a mailbox or in local
storage. The GDPR interface is GOV-07’s: retention under this section is a legal obligation and comes
before erasure requests for the retention period. Conflicts are handled per GOV-07, never case by
case by the analyst.


12 · Deviations
No one may deviate from the prohibited list (C-6, GOV-04). Any other deviation from this work
procedure requires four things before it happens: the MLRO’s written approval, a stated reason, a
compensating control, and an entry in the deviation register. The register records: date, file, the
requirement deviated from, the reason, the compensating control, the approver, and the closure. The
MLRO reviews the register quarterly and reports it to the board in the MLRO report.

A deviation is approved in advance. The same act without prior approval is an incident under
GOV-02, regardless of how it turned out, and discovering one is itself a section 13 finding. Repeated
deviation requests against the same requirement mean that either the requirement or the operation
is wrong. That goes to SOP-25 as a proposed change, not into a growing register.


13 · Quality control
Second review: every high-risk approval, every PEP file and every S4.5 escalation receives a second
review before the decision is communicated. The second reviewer is never the deciding analyst, and
records the review in Kompass.

Monthly re-review: every month, 10% of low-risk approvals and 20% of medium-risk approvals are
re-reviewed against this work procedure. The selection is drawn randomly and the draw is recorded.

Findings are graded in three levels. Observation: the documentation falls short of the standard, but
the decision is sound. Finding: a step was not performed to standard. Critical: a decision is
unsupported, or a mandatory step was skipped. A critical reopens the file and reaches the MLRO
within 24 hours. A finding is corrected within the month. Observations feed the training programme.

The monthly quality-control report shows counts by grade, themes, the distribution per step, and
correction status. Two consecutive months of the same theme trigger a thematic review of every
open file touching that step. Repeated findings of the same kind trigger a revision of this work
procedure through SOP-25.


14 · Special situations

14.1 Additions to an existing customer group
Where an applicant belongs to a group Mynta has already onboarded, group-level evidence may be
reused, but only under two conditions: it meets C-1 age limits at the time of this application, and the



Mynta AB | SOP-10 | v2.0 sample, plain register                                               Page 12 of 17
ownership above the joining company is unchanged. "Unchanged" is verified against current registry
evidence. It is never assumed from the group file. The scope for the new company is: full S1 to S3
and S5 to S8, plus S4 from the joining company up to the first level that is already documented fresh
in the group file. The joining company’s risk rating is at least the group’s, and higher where its own
factors require it. The group file and the new file refer to each other.

14.2 Re-application after decline, exit or abandonment
The declined and abandoned registers are checked at S1. A re-application from a declined or risk-
exited company, or from a company sharing UBOs with one, is owned by a senior analyst from intake.
The prior file is retrieved and read before any new assessment starts. The new analysis must state,
specifically, what has changed since the prior outcome. Where the prior outcome was an exit for risk,
or a decline with SOP-20 relevance, the MLRO must approve before processing begins at all.
Nothing about the prior assessment is revealed to the applicant at any point.

14.3 Restructuring during onboarding
Any change to the ownership structure during the process invalidates every S4 conclusion. S4 re-
runs from S4.1, with fresh evidence for every affected level, and the decision clock resets. A structure
that changes while under review, particularly toward spreading below the thresholds, is recorded as
a red flag (Appendix F) and escalated, with the before-and-after structures documented side by side.

14.4 Withdrawal and abandonment
An applicant may withdraw at any time. The file closes as withdrawn and is kept per section 11. An
application with an information request unanswered for 30 days closes as abandoned, after one
recorded reminder. In both cases the analyst assesses whether the attempted relationship must be
reported per SOP-20, exactly as for a decline, and the company is recorded in the abandoned
register checked at S1. One pattern deserves attention: withdrawal right after a discrepancy was put
to the customer. That is itself an indicator, and it is recorded for SOP-20 assessment.


15 · Country handling
A country connection means any of three things: the country of registration of any company in the
ownership structure; the main place of operations; or the residence of any beneficial owner.
Citizenship alone is not a connection. Residence is.

The handling follows rule C-2, which places countries in categories. Standard category: normal
handling. Elevated category: closer document checks, and mandatory verification of the ownership
level that decides the outcome against a second source. High-risk category: SOP-13 always, and the
MLRO decides, regardless of the SOP-14 score. Prohibited country, at any level and whenever
discovered: decline, per the S1.3 handling, and GOV-03 is assessed where the country overlaps a
sanctions programme. If a prohibited connection is discovered after approval, that is an SOP-22
event trigger and a candidate for exit under SOP-23.

17 · Systems and recording
Kompass, Mynta’s case management system, is the system of record for this work procedure. Every
section 6 step has a Kompass checkpoint. The checkpoint is completed, with its evidence attached,
before the next step opens. Evidence uploads carry source and date per 7.4. Screening results attach
through the SOP-12 integration; a manual attachment is logged as an exception to the normal flow.
Decisions are taken in the Kompass workflow, at the C-6 level. An approval given by email or chat
message does not exist and is invalid. The audit trail cannot be edited. Corrections are made by
adding a new entry, never by changing an old one. A registry lookup performed outside Kompass is
documented by an export or a capture per 7.4, and filed the same day.


18 · Training and competence
An analyst completes the KYB module of the training programme before deciding files alone, and the
annual refresher after that. The training register documents both. Senior-analyst authority requires at
least two years of onboarding casework and the MLRO’s sign-off. Section 13 quality-control findings
feed the training content through the MLRO report. If the same finding repeats for the same person,
that person’s solo decision authority is paused until re-training is documented.

Appendix G · Worked example: complete versus defective file
Applicant: Lagerkrans Interiör AB, a Swedish AB selling furniture online, applying for an account and
acquiring. A complete file at S1 looks like this. The application is signed by the registered signatory.
T-1 declares one UBO at 100%. The chart matches. The Bolagsverket e-extract is dated within C-1.
The aktiebok names the UBO. The signatory-UBO is verified with BankID. The business description
states expected volumes per product area. The MCC is 5712, with a live website passing Appendix D
checks 1 to 5. The file proceeds to S2 the same day.

Now the same file with three typical defects, and the required handling. First: the extract is 8 months
old. The item fails C-1. Request fresh evidence, pause the clock, and do not begin the assessment
(S1.2). Second: the aktiebok names two holders at 50% each, while T-1 declares one at 100%. That is
a discrepancy on an ownership fact. S4.5 applies in full: memo per T-2, no proceeding, no declining,
and no silent correction, even though the fix seems obvious. Third: expected acquiring volume of
SEK 4m per month, with no trading history and a website created last month. That is not reasonable,
and S3.2 applies. Document it and escalate with the file. Do not resolve it by taking the customer’s
word.

Each defect is handled by the step that owns it. Putting the requests into one message to the
customer is fine. Deciding anything before the defects are resolved is not.
