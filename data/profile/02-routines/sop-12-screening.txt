1 · Purpose and scope
This routine sets how persons and entities connected to Mynta are screened against sanctions lists,
PEP data and adverse media, and how matches are decided. The designated adverse-media
categories cover money laundering, terrorist financing, sanctions evasion, corruption, tax crime,
fraud, cybercrime, trafficking and other serious predicate offences; the set may be expanded risk-
based, with the expansion recorded. It covers three things. Name screening at onboarding. Ongoing
and event-driven screening of the existing customer base. And the standard for deciding on hits
used everywhere, including for transaction-level hits routed from SOP-18 and wallet hits routed from
SOP-17. Two things are out of scope. Who must be screened in a given case is set by the referring
routine, on the identified-not-declared rule. Screening beyond the required population is personal-
data processing without an automatic legal ground: it requires control indications or an approved
deviation, and is never run just to be safe. The freeze itself is governed by SOP-21.

3 · Definitions

Hit                        Any vendor-generated potential match at or above the C-4 similarity threshold
                           (85%).

Secondary identifiers      Date of birth, nationality or country of registration, identifiers such as
                           personnummer or org.nr, and address, in that order of weight.

Dismiss                    A recorded determination that a hit is not the listed person, on the C-4 evidence
                           standard.

True match                 A hit confirmed as the listed person or entity, or one that cannot be dismissed on
                           evidence.

Delta screening            Re-screening the full active base against list changes upon every update.



4 · When screening runs
S1.1       Onboarding: every person and entity referred by SOP-10 S5.1 or SOP-11, before any product
           activation. The file is not complete until the screening results are on it.

S1.2       Ongoing: delta screening of the full active base on every list update, within the GOV-03
           SLA; results first sortingd the same day for sanctions deltas.

S1.3       Event-driven: whenever a routine identifies a new person in a relationship (ownership
           change, new signatory, SOP-22 findings), that person is screened before the event
           assessment closes.

S1.4       Transaction-level: payment counterparty hits from SOP-18 and wallet hits from SOP-17 arrive
           as the decision on hits referrals and follow sections 5 and 6 with sanctions timing.

5 · Decision on hits
S2.1       Every hit is decided individually; batch dismissing by pattern is prohibited. The person
           deciding works from the hit record, the person's file data and, where needed, registry
           evidence, never from customer assurances.

S2.2       Dismiss at first line IF the identifier evidence is strong enough: one strong mismatch, such
           as date of birth against a verified document, may be sufficient, while two weak mismatches
           may not be. The strength assessment and the rationale are recorded per hit. One weak
           mismatched identifier alone never suffices; name-only similarity with no secondary data
           shall be escalated, not dismissed.

S2.3       IF a sanctions hit cannot be dismissed on S2.2's standard, THEN treat it as a true match
           immediately. SOP-21 applies at once. The relationship is suspended, and the customer is not
           notified. Analysis continues after the freeze. That ordering comes from GOV-03 §8.

S2.4       IF a PEP hit is confirmed or cannot be dismissed, THEN the referring file re-routes per
           SOP-10 §8: high risk, SOP-13 measures, MLRO authority. A customer's denial of PEP status
           against a confirmed function match is a discrepancy, not a dismiss.

S2.5       IF an adverse-media hit in a designated category is confirmed as the person, THEN route to
           SOP-13 per C-9; record the credibility of the source and how specific the allegation is. That
           assessment belongs to the EDD scoping. It is not a reason to drop the hit at screening.

S2.6       Escalation: any decision on hits the first line cannot close on evidence goes to the screening
           specialist; sanctions doubt goes to the MLRO with SOP-21 already applied. Timelines per
           C-5: sanctions immediate, PEP and adverse media within 3 business days.


6 · Recording
Keep one record per screened person. It contains: the lists and list versions screened against, the
date and vendor reference, every hit with its decision, reason and decision-maker, and the person's
current net status. This record is what SOP-10 S5.4 files and what QC samples. If a person's
screening status cannot be read in one place, that person counts as unscreened for decision
purposes.


7 · System governance
Threshold and fuzzy-matching configuration implements C-4 and changes only through recorded
change control with before-and-after test evidence. Annual effectiveness testing covers list
coverage, update latency, known-entity seeding (planted test names must hit), and dismiss-quality
re-review of a random selection: 5% of first-line dismiss re-reviewed monthly by the screening
specialist, findings graded per the SOP-10 §13 scale. Before any tuning that reduces hit volume is
approved, its effect on recall shall be tested and recorded.


8 · Records and review
Records per GOV-02 §10. This routine is reviewed annually, on vendor or configuration change, and
on GOV-03 revisions.
