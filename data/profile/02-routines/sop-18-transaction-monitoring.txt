1 · Purpose and scope
This instruction sets out how customer activity is monitored across all four product areas: accounts
and payments, card issuing, merchant acquiring, and crypto Crypto Service activity. It defines the
monitoring architecture, the rule families per product area, the alert standard, and the governance of
rules and parameters. It applies to all customers and all products from activation. Investigation of
alerts is governed by SOP-19.


2 · Architecture
Monitoring operates on three inputs: transaction data per product area, the customer's baseline
recorded at onboarding (SOP-10 section 16, SOP-11 S7.2), and the customer's risk class per SOP-14.
Rules run continuously or in daily batch per the rule register. Each rule evaluates against the
customer's total position across all product areas as well as the triggering product area. Per GOV-01
conclusion C5, a monitoring configuration that evaluates product areas in isolation only is a control
failure and shall be reported as an incident.

3 · Rule families per product area

the rule register with per-risk-class values. Rule identifiers follow the pattern R-[product area]-

5 · New-account regime
For the register-defined initial period after activation, customers run tightened parameters on R-
ACC-01, R-ACC-02, R-CRD-02 and R-CRY-02, and any first occurrence of the high-risk category
cross-border flow activity or Crypto Service use alerts regardless of value. If an alert during the
period closes with a note, the period extends automatically. Otherwise the customer leaves the
regime automatically when the period ends.

6 · Alert standard
Every alert delivered to SOP-19 shall contain: the rule ID and version, the triggering data with values
against parameters, the customer identifier and risk class, the relevant baseline fields, a link to total
cross-product area activity for the window, and prior alert history on the customer. An alert missing
any of these is a data incident and shall be logged as such. Sanctions-linked alerts route
simultaneously to SOP-21 handling.


7 · Data completeness
Monitoring depends on complete feeds from all four product areas and on baseline data. Feed
interruptions, schema changes and missing-field rates above register tolerances shall be detected
by automated reconciliation, logged as incidents, and reported to the MLRO the same day.
Transactions arriving during an interruption shall be monitored retroactively on restoration, and the
retroactive run shall be documented.


8 · Rule governance and tuning
The monitoring rule register is the system of record for rules, parameters, per-class values, versions
and owners. Changes follow change control: proposal with rationale and expected effect, MLRO
approval, pre-implementation testing on historical data, implementation, and a post-implementation
check after one full cycle. Emergency additions in response to an active typology may be
implemented on MLRO approval with testing completed within ten business days.

Tuning inputs: the SOP-19 closing decision tags (true concern, reasonable but benign, rule noise),
per-rule alert and conversion statistics, and QC findings. Parameter changes that reduce alert
volume shall be tested for their effect on detection of known cases before approval. Suppression of
a rule for a named customer requires senior-analyst proposal, MLRO approval, a recorded reason, an
expiry date, and quarterly review of all live suppressions.


9 · Effectiveness testing
Annually, and after material rule changes: above-the-line and below-the-line testing on samples
around current parameters; seeding of known typology patterns through the test environment with
expected alerts verified; coverage mapping of the GOV-01 Appendix B typologies against live rules,
with uncovered typologies logged and corrected; and review of the alert-to-outcome funnel per rule.
Results are reported in the MLRO report with correction owners and dates.


10 · Records and review
Alert data, rule versions, parameter histories, suppressions, incidents and test results follow GOV-02
section 10 retention. This instruction is reviewed annually and on GOV-01 revisions. The rule register
is reviewed quarterly by the MLRO.
