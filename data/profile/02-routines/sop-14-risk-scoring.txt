1 · Purpose and principles
The score turns GOV-01's institutional risk understanding into a per-customer classification: low,
medium or high. The classification drives verification depth, decision authority, monitoring intensity
and review frequency. It must therefore be reproducible: same inputs, same class, no matter who
computes it. That is why the factors, values and arithmetic are fixed in this document and recorded
per customer (SOP-10 S6.3). Judgement is applied through the override mechanism in section 5. Ad
hoc re-weighting of factors at the desk is prohibited.


2 · Structure
The model is an additive factor score, with mandatory-high triggers on top. It works in four steps.
Each factor takes the points of its highest applicable row. The factor points are added into a raw
score. The raw score maps to a class through the bands in section 4. The mandatory triggers then
force high, regardless of the arithmetic. There is no channel factor. Distribution is remote for every
customer (GOV-01 §8), and a factor that is the same for everyone separates no one.

Mandatory high regardless of score: any PEP connection per SOP-10 §8; any high-risk-category
connection; any confirmed designated adverse media; any C-9 trigger not already carried by a
factor; and any case where GOV-01 rates the customer's defining feature high while the arithmetic
lands lower. That last rule is the reconciliation that GOV-01 conclusion C8 requires. Every mandatory-
high entry records which trigger fired.


5 · Overrides
Only downward overrides need this process, and they are rare by design. A downward override
requires four things: MLRO approval; a written rationale explaining why the triggering factor
overstates this customer's risk; an entry in the override register; and a 12-month review cycle,
regardless of the resulting class. Upward judgement is not an override. Any analyst may classify
above the arithmetic, with the reason recorded. If overrides keep hitting the same factor, that factor
goes into the section 7 validation, per GOV-01 C8. The model shall be corrected on evidence;
classifications shall not be adjusted to fit the model.

6 · Worked example
Halvix-pattern applicant: foreign-layer legal person (8), two-layer ownership structure at declaration
but three effective layers once recalculated (12), the elevated category connection through the
Estonian holdco (8), applying for accounts and acquiring (8), standard industry (0), expected activity
above norm with partial support (4), no crypto (0). Raw score 40: high, before any trigger. The MLRO
decides. In this pattern, the S4.5 discrepancy handling is already running for its own reasons. The
score shall be computed on the facts as established under SOP-10 S4, not on the customer's
declaration.


7 · Validation and change control
Annually, and after material GOV-01 revisions: factor and band performance is validated against
outcomes, class distribution across the book, escalation and SAR rates per class, override frequency
and direction, and misclassification found in QC. Bands should separate outcomes; a model whose
high class contains no more findings than its medium class is recalibrated. Changes to factors,
values or bands go through recorded change control with a GOV-01 basis stated, apply prospectively,
and trigger rescoring of affected segments at their next review, or immediately where the change
closes an identified gap.
