1 · Purpose and scope
Every signal that needs examining flows through this routine. It owns the path from signal to closing
decision. The examination duty in GOV-02 §9 is discharged here. That sets the quality bar: closing an
alert without a genuine examination is a breach of GOV-02 section 9.2. The routine also owns the
internal reporting channel. Any employee, in any role, can raise a suspicion here or directly with the
MLRO, using a simple dedicated form, the customer, the facts, the transactions, the urgency and the
supporting documents, kept separate from the whistleblowing channel, is logged like any other
intake, and carries protection against reprisal per the whistleblowing procedure.


2 · Definitions and closing decisions

Alert                        Any signal requiring examination, whatever its source.

Case                         One or more linked alerts on one customer or connected customers, examined
                             together.

Closing decisions            Close, no finding; close with note (fact recorded to the profile, no suspicion); refer
                             to SOP-13, SOP-22 or SOP-23; refer to the MLRO for SOP-20 assessment;
                             SOP-21 immediate (sanctions). Every closing decision carries a written rationale.

3 · Procedure
S1         First sorting. Within the priority SLA (section 5): deduplicate, link to the customer and any
           related open items, and set priority: sanctions-linked immediate, active-fraud-linked urgent
           (same day), all else standard. IF the alert concerns a file the analyst personally onboarded or
           decided, THEN it is reassigned; nobody examines their own decisions.

S2         Context assembly. Pull the monitoring baseline (SOP-10 §16 data), the due-diligence file,
           screening records, prior alerts and their closing decisions, and activity across all product
           areas per GOV-01 conclusion C5. The closing options are: duplicate alert; false alert;
           explained, with the explanation verified; profile update required; fraud referral; system error;
           and suspicion, routed to SOP-20. An examination performed on the triggering transaction
           alone, without the total view, does not meet the standard.

S3         Examination. Establish what actually happened and whether it makes sense against the
           customer's profile and the typology the rule encodes. The record separates facts (each with
           its source) from assessment. Findings of "unusual but explained" name the explanation and
           its evidence; "explained by the customer" without confirmation by a second source is
           recorded as exactly that.

S4         Customer contact. Requests for information are permitted and often necessary; where the
           contact itself could create a tipping-off risk, the MLRO is consulted first; they are worded
           neutrally (documentation requests, activity clarification), never disclosing examination,
           suspicion or reporting, per meddelandeförbudet. Deadlines are set and logged. IF the
           customer does not respond by the deadline plus one reminder, THEN the non-response
           becomes a finding in its own right and routes to SOP-22 or SOP-23 assessment.

S5         Second review. Second review before closure for: repeated alerts on the same customer,
           connected customers, large transactions, cases closed mainly on the customer’s
           explanation, and any case touching a high-risk or PEP relationship, any case with a prior SAR
           on the customer, any close-no-finding on an urgent-priority case, and any closing decision
           the analyst marks uncertain. The reviewer is never the examining analyst.

S6         Closing decision and routing. Close or route per section 2. IF examination leaves reasonable
           grounds to suspect, or cannot dispel them, THEN the case goes to the MLRO for SOP-20
           assessment; uncertainty after examination is a reason to escalate, not to close. Referrals
           carry the assembled context, so the receiving routine does not start from zero.

S7         Feedback. Every closing decision tags alert quality (true concern, reasonable but benign,
           rule noise); the tags are added up monthly and feed SOP-18 tuning through its change
           control. This feedback loop is what keeps alert quality workable at Mynta's volumes.


4 · Case records
Per case: intake source and time, priority, context assembled, facts with sources, customer contacts
verbatim, assessment, closing decision with rationale, reviewer where S5 applied, and routing. The
reconstruction standard from SOP-10 S7.2 applies: a reader with the record alone can follow the
reasoning to the closing decision.

5 · Timelines and queue governance
The first review target is 3 business days, and sooner is better. Once suspicion is formed, reporting
follows SOP-20 without delay. No case ages past 15 days without escalation to the MLRO, and
overdue cases escalate from the due date.

Queue health (volumes, aging, closing decision mix, per-rule noise rates) is reviewed weekly by the
senior analysts and monthly in the MLRO report; sustained backlog shall be escalated to
management as a resourcing decision and shall not reduce examination quality.


6 · Quality control
Monthly re-review of a random selection: 10% of close-no-finding closing decisions re-examined by
a senior analyst not involved in them, graded on the SOP-10 §13 scale; all reopened cases logged
with cause. Grading looks for the two failure modes in order of harm: closes that missed the point,
and escalations that merely moved work without examination. Findings feed training and, where a
rule generates systematic noise, SOP-18 tuning.
